# Board standard

Use this standard to develop the first moodboards after the person confirms the brief.
Preserve their visual richness and the qualities retained through the interview.

## Direction and source evidence

Hold the product, audience, platform, content, and shared task set constant across directions.
Keep state facts consistent, but let grouping, proportions, representations, and board composition vary with each visual thesis.
Comparable task coverage does not require identical widgets, device mockups, or sample positions.
Keep differences in features separate from differences in visual direction; do not add unique analytics or workflows to make one board seem richer.
Write one direction packet per moodboard before composition, each with a distinct visual thesis and hierarchy strategy.
Describe how typography, imagery, color, material, shape, space, and product fragments express that thesis together.
Carry forward the selected reference worlds and their observed qualities from the first exploration.
Record how each world supports the shared tasks, what it contributes beyond familiar category styling, and where it risks reducing clarity.
Develop breadth across tasks before refining the composition of an individual screen.
Ground choices in the brief and observed reference qualities. Mark inherited brand elements and fixed boundaries.
Turn the person's exclusions into explicit review criteria.

Inspect supplied references first. Gather relevant references from several fields, including sources outside the product category.
Choose imagery for its subject, viewpoint, crop, light, texture, and narrative role.
Record the source URL or supplied file, its creator when known, and the quality each reference contributes.
Use only material that the available tools permit you to capture and display.

Before assembly, capture or retrieve permitted reference images into local assets and inspect each image.
A search thumbnail, source URL, successful download command, or image description does not prove that an asset loaded.
Use those inspected assets in the artifact so that its imagery does not depend on remote hotlinks.
If an asset cannot be captured or inspected, report it and stop under the skill's presentation gate.
Never label a generated image or vector reconstruction as evidence from an unavailable source.

Generate original material to fill a defined gap or explore a combination that the references do not supply.
Describe the subject, viewpoint, crop, light, texture, palette, and role before generation.
Pass inspected reference images when the tool supports them, stating which observed qualities to carry forward.
Label generated material as original exploration, separate from sourced evidence.
Generated type and swatches remain illustrative until reconstructed in the foundation.

Done when each direction has its own packet and all its image assets are inspectable, with honest provenance.

## Compose the review artifact

Use one review artifact containing the brief, an overview, every full board, a comparison, and a feedback area.
Keep the decision record pending until approval.
Give each full board the same readable canvas size and evidence coverage, with its own composition and visual hierarchy.
Use an anchor image, supporting groups, scale, cropping, and space to express the direction itself.
The overview must allow comparison, and each full board must remain readable on its own.

Curate 10 to 15 distinct visual samples per direction, without padding the count with repeated subjects.
Samples include image references, type specimens, palette relationships, material studies, graphic forms, and product fragments.
Give the strongest specimen the greatest visual weight, using the product-specific priorities in the skill.
For software, draw most samples from interface structure, typography, controls, states, and functional surfaces.
Use photography and physical materials only when their treatment contributes to the product.
Make all ten evidence areas visible:

1. Direction: Thesis, project goal, and defining qualities.
2. Anchor: The strongest interface specimen for software, or the strongest visual reference for an image-led surface.
3. Image treatment: Functional imagery or illustration where relevant, including its role, crop, and treatment.
4. Color behavior: Relationships, proportions, contrast, and surfaces, supported by a palette.
5. Typography: Display and reading samples with hierarchy, weight, scale, and measure.
6. Composition: Alignment, density, whitespace, cropping, and focus.
7. Shape: Geometry, edges, borders, lines, icons, patterns, and marks.
8. Surfaces: Layers, borders, depth, shadow, translucency, texture, or deliberate flatness in the actual product.
9. Product translation: Several interface fragments with shared product content, navigation, controls, and relevant interaction states.
10. Rationale: Short annotations connecting each evidence cluster to a quality the product can use.

Keep product fragments small enough that the board remains a moodboard rather than a complete screen design.
Give them enough space for close inspection of text alignment, hierarchy, padding, and control geometry.
If imagery has no functional role, state that boundary and use the space for additional interface evidence.
Choose specific imagery and typography that contribute to the thesis, rather than filling a standard board template.
Preserve the references' expressive qualities through deliberate typography, colour, imagery, geometry, and composition in the review artifact.
Treat sample coverage as a minimum, not evidence of beauty or completeness.
Prioritize a coherent, exploratory composition over a tiled specification sheet or a finished screen surrounded by swatches.
Use reference-derived details to shape the interface itself; decorative props alone cannot carry a product direction.
Add motion, sound, illustration systems, or data visualization when they carry product meaning.
End each direction with what it optimizes, gives up, risks, and conflicts with.

Done when each direction contains all ten evidence areas and 10 to 15 useful samples.

## Inspect the set

Apply the skill's presentation gate to all renders.
Inspect every full board and the overview at presentation size, including how the artifact is viewed on desktop and mobile.
For a fixed canvas, provide readable full-size frames instead of compressing all boards into one tiny image.
Record the visible evidence for each required area and the sample count for each direction.
Correct broken images, unreadable labels, clipping, missing evidence, unsupported choices, and violations of the person's exclusions.

Compare every pair across typography, imagery, composition, color behavior, material, shape, and product translation.
For each dimension, name the actual visible difference or record that it is unchanged.
Every pair must differ in typography, composition, color behavior, and surface treatment, plus imagery when the product uses it.
Render a comparison with hidden names, one in grayscale, and blurred thumbnails.
Inspect these renders: hierarchy, silhouette, image treatment, and space must distinguish every pair without labels or accent colors.
Show these comparison results with the artifact so the person can inspect the evidence.
If a pair collapses into one visual idea, revise its structural choices and repeat the affected comparison.
A written score is not a substitute for the rendered evidence.

Done when all coverage and comparison claims are supported by inspected renders.

## Present for feedback

Open the artifact in a visible preview or show its rendered frames inline, following the skill's presentation gate.
Show the overview, each readable full board, and the comparison before requesting a direction decision.
Explain each thesis, strongest quality, and tradeoff, then ask what attracts and repels the person.
Keep recommendations provisional and preserve the person's ability to combine specific qualities.
Leave the decision record pending until a visible revision receives explicit approval.

## Method reference

[Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable) informs the reference selection and interface craft approach.
This workflow uses equal-weight, cross-task moodboards in place of its screen-composition and implementation sequence.
